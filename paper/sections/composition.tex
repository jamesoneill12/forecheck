\section{Multi-policy composition, corrected}
\label{sec:composition}

Guardrail deployments run several policies and act on the most restrictive
decision. We stack $k$ bundles over the v2 decoder's probability vector on
\texttt{heldout\_family} ($n{=}6640$) two ways: \texttt{independent}
evaluates each bundle alone and ORs the decisions; \texttt{expected\_cost}
merges the bundles' rules and decides once by the argmin of expected cost
over the joint vector under a fixed cost table
(Figure~\ref{fig:stacking-body}; Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-stacking}). FPR
counts any non-\textsc{allow} decision on a covered benign example, so it
includes \textsc{review}. An earlier draft read these numbers as ``threshold
stacking compounds to a 0.97 false positive rate.'' It does not.

\textbf{The $k{=}3$ jump is one fail-closed bundle.} FPR goes 0.015, 0.140,
0.966 and stays there through fifteen bundles. The third bundle,
\texttt{conservative}, has \texttt{default\_decision: review}: alone it
routes essentially every covered call to a human, so the OR saturates when it
enters and nothing added afterwards changes it; independent guards at the
observed $k{=}1$ rates would compound to about 0.04. One fail-closed policy
dominates an OR-stack, which XACML's \texttt{deny-overrides} and Cedar's
forbid-trumps-permit semantics \citep{oasis2013xacml,cutler2024cedar} make
explicit. \texttt{expected\_cost} is constant from $k{=}2$ because the merged
rule set already covers every dimension the stack will ever cover. Its level
differs by arm (decoder 0.082 vs.\ encoder 0.921), but the arms also differ
in discrimination (granite-r2 0.433 on \texttt{policy\_conflict} vs.\ 0.964),
so we do not attribute that gap to calibration. The approval-elimination
curve on the same probabilities is in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-approval}.
