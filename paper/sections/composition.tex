\section{Multi-policy composition, corrected}
\label{sec:composition}

Guardrail deployments run several policies and act on the most restrictive
decision. We stack $k$ bundles over the v2 decoder's probability vector on
\texttt{heldout\_family} ($n{=}6640$) two ways: \texttt{independent}
evaluates each bundle alone and ORs the decisions; \texttt{expected\_cost}
merges the bundles' rules and decides once by the argmin of expected cost
over the joint vector under a fixed cost table
(Table~\ref{tab:stacking-main}; Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-stacking}). FPR
counts any non-\textsc{allow} decision on a covered benign example, so it
includes \textsc{review}. An earlier draft read this table as ``threshold
stacking compounds to a 0.97 false positive rate by three policies.'' It
does not.

\textbf{The $k{=}3$ jump is one fail-closed bundle.} FPR goes 0.015, 0.140,
0.966 and stays there through fifteen bundles. The third bundle,
\texttt{conservative}, has \texttt{default\_decision: review}: alone it
routes essentially every covered call to a human, so the OR saturates when it
enters and the eleven single-dimension policies added afterwards change
nothing. Independent guards at the observed $k{=}1$ rates would compound to
about 0.04. The lesson is that one fail-closed policy dominates an OR-stack,
which XACML's \texttt{deny-overrides} and Cedar's forbid-trumps-permit
semantics \citep{oasis2013xacml,cutler2024cedar} make explicit; naive OR is
not the alternative a practitioner would deploy.

\textbf{Expected-cost invariance in $k$ is structural.} From $k{=}2$ the
merged rule set covers every dimension the stack will ever cover
(covered-benign count constant at 2,790) and duplicate rules are merged, so
the decision is provably constant in $k$. Adding a restrictive bundle on top
of two others then changes nothing, which a policy author may not want.
Calibration sets the level of the constant line (decoder 0.082 vs.\ encoder
0.921), but the arms also differ in discrimination (granite-r2 scores 0.433
on \texttt{policy\_conflict} against the decoder's 0.964), so we do not
attribute the gap to calibration; a within-arm temperature perturbation would
settle it and has not been run. At $k{=}1$ the two rules are different operating
points (FPR 0.058 vs.\ 0.015, FNR 0.170 vs.\ 0.253), and by the cost the
rule optimises the $k{=}8$ gap is 10\%. The approval-elimination curve on
the same probabilities is in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-approval}.
