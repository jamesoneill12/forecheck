\section{Identity ablation as a diagnostic}
\label{sec:identity}

At evaluation time only, we remove principal entitlements, delegated scope,
\texttt{on\_behalf\_of}, and policy text from the trained decoder's rendered
context (\texttt{--strip-identity}), leaving action, destination, and
trajectory untouched. Three labels are deterministic functions of the removed
fields, so their collapse is expected; the ablation is informative about
(i) how much the remaining content predicts them, (ii) whether anything else
moves, and (iii) whether arms that keep the context use it. We compare
against the rule baseline, a decoder \emph{trained} on identity-free
renderings, the same ablation at 8B, and the zero-shot arms
(Table~\ref{tab:macro-summary}, Figure~\ref{fig:identity-body}; v2 and all
dimensions in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-identity}).

\textbf{Residual content signal is small on two of the three dimensions.}
Stripped, \texttt{unauthorized\_scope} sits at 0.125 against a 0.108 positive
rate and \texttt{policy\_conflict} at 0.392 against 0.371: without the
entitlements or the clause, the call text carries almost nothing about
either. \texttt{insufficient\_context} retains 0.412 against 0.087, well above
chance; a blind reader cannot recover this label from the text at all
(\S\ref{sec:labelvalidity}), so the residual is a generator regularity.

\textbf{Nothing else moves, but most of it could not.} The eight remaining
dimensions change by at most 0.002. Five are at 1.000 for the checker, three
of those for the rule baseline too, so ``content dimensions are unaffected''
is largely a ceiling effect. \texttt{privilege\_escalation} is unaffected
because the v4 renderer writes the deciding authority fields into the call
(before v4 it scored 0.46--0.53); the v4 injection score is unaffected because
its evidence was a leaked argument key in the call (\S\ref{sec:external}).

\textbf{Training without the fields gives the same ceiling.} A decoder
trained on identity-free renderings reaches macro 0.784 against 0.790 for
eval-time stripping, within 0.06 per dimension, so the drop is not a broken
shortcut or a prompt-format shift; 8B collapses identically (0.958 to 0.786).

\textbf{The zero-shot arms are at chance everywhere, not selectively.} Given
the same policy text as a custom criterion, Granite Guardian and
gpt-oss-safeguard score 0.26--0.28 macro, below the rule baseline's 0.614,
and within 0.09 of the positive rate on ten of eleven dimensions each,
including content dimensions the rule baseline solves exactly
(Table~\ref{tab:zeroshot-perdim}); the exceptions are Guardian on
\texttt{financial\_commitment} (0.38 against 0.09) and gpt-oss-safeguard on
\texttt{policy\_conflict} (0.49 against 0.29, $n{=}103$). We validated the
chat-template arguments only against test doubles, so we cannot separate
``these models lack authorization reasoning'' from ``this prompt does not
reach it.'' The supportable claim is narrow: an off-the-shelf guardrail given
the policy as a custom criterion does not answer these questions here.
