\section{Identity ablation}
\label{sec:identity}

At evaluation time only, we remove principal entitlements, delegated scope,
\texttt{on\_behalf\_of}, and policy text from the trained decoder's rendered
context (\texttt{--strip-identity}), leaving action, destination, and
trajectory untouched, and compare against a rule baseline (action and
destination fields only) and two zero-shot safety models given the same
policy text in-prompt.

Table~\ref{tab:macro-summary}: identity-aware trained models lose
0.10--0.18 macro AUPRC when identity is removed; the rule baseline is
unchanged; the zero-shot models are flat or slightly \emph{improve}. If a
general safety model quietly used delegated-authority context, stripping it
would hurt -- it does not, for either model.

The v4 ablation replicates the v2 pattern on the fixed data: macro AUPRC drops
0.960 to 0.790, close to v2's 0.915 to 0.736. The one change is
\texttt{privilege\_escalation}, which now survives stripping entirely (1.000
to 1.000): the v4 render fix makes \texttt{authority\_before}/\texttt{authority\_after}
observable directly in the tool-call text, so it is a content dimension, not
an identity one. An 8B decoder on the same v4 data shows the identical collapse
(0.958 to 0.786, Table~\ref{tab:macro-summary}), with the same three dimensions
falling and the same two surviving -- model scale gives the classifier no way to
infer entitlements or policy text that were simply not in its input.

Training on data rendered without identity fields from the start, rather than
stripping only at evaluation time, gives the same ceiling: macro AUPRC
0.780/0.784 on test/heldout\_family, within a point of the eval-time-stripped
model's 0.787/0.790. The three delegated-authority dimensions still do not
recover: heldout\_family \texttt{unauthorized\_scope} falls to 0.107,
\texttt{policy\_conflict} to 0.429, \texttt{insufficient\_context} to 0.357,
matching or below the eval-time-stripped model and nowhere near the full
model's 0.999/0.964/0.841. This rules out the reading that eval-time stripping
merely broke a fragile mechanism the model had learned to use -- the
information is absent from the rendered content itself.

Figure~\ref{fig:identity-ablation} shows the same pattern across all eleven
dimensions: the drop from stripping identity is concentrated in
\texttt{unauthorized\_scope}, \texttt{policy\_conflict}, and
\texttt{insufficient\_context}, while the remaining dimensions barely move.

The macro drop is concentrated entirely in the three delegated-authority
dimensions. \texttt{unauthorized\_scope} falls below the rule baseline
(0.116 vs.\ 0.512) once the entitlements it must be checked against are
gone; \texttt{policy\_conflict} falls to 0.345, barely above the 0.315 rule
floor, because there is no clause left to violate; \texttt{insufficient\_context}
falls by half. The fourth row is untouched: its deciding fact renders
directly into the action arguments regardless of identity, and the rule
baseline already gets it right. Four more dimensions
(\texttt{destructive\_or\_irreversible\_action}, \texttt{untrusted\_destination},
\texttt{external\_communication}, \texttt{sensitive\_data\_exposure}) show
the same flat, lookup pattern; two more
(\texttt{prompt\_injection\_influence} 0.810, \texttt{suspicious\_action\_sequence}
0.952) are learned from trajectory order and are identity-independent.

The zero-shot models' near-chance macro AUPRC (0.24--0.27), unmoved by
identity, shows this is not a prompting gap: both were trained for harm,
jailbreak, and groundedness criteria, not for checking an action against
entitlements or policy. gpt-oss-safeguard is also $\sim$50$\times$ slower
per row than the 2B decoder, for no benefit on the dimensions that matter
here.

This whole claim rests on the three delegated-authority dimensions being real
labels, not generator artifacts an independent reader cannot recover;
\S\ref{sec:labelvalidity} checks that directly and finds the claim solid for
two of the three, with \texttt{insufficient\_context} unvalidated.
