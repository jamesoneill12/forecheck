\section{Transfer to data we did not generate}
\label{sec:external}

We score the checkers zero-shot on two published benchmarks. Nothing was
trained or tuned on either; calibration was fitted on synthetic dev and
threshold selection is disabled.

\textbf{AgentDojo} \citep{debenedetti2024agentdojo}: banking-suite traces for
claude-3-5-sonnet-20241022 and gpt-4o-2024-05-13, 1,714 runs, 4,215 proposed
calls, 11 attack types plus no-attack runs. Labels come from AgentDojo's own
\texttt{ground\_truth()} functions: \texttt{prompt\_injection\_influence} is
\texttt{yes} when a call executes the injection task's goal (positive rate
0.150); \texttt{unauthorized\_scope} when the call's function is outside the
user task's reference solution (0.253); \texttt{policy\_conflict} when one of
four banking policies we wrote once fires under a deterministic predicate
(0.237). \textbf{InjecAgent} \citep{zhan2024injecagent}: 1,054 cases over 38
toolkits. AgentDojo's injection positives differ from negatives in the call
itself (an unrequested transfer to a recipient the user never named), so it
cannot tell whether a checker reads the untrusted text or the call. InjecAgent
can: for each attacker tool we build a \emph{poisoned/clean pair}, the same
attacker call with identical arguments, proposed after the tool response with
the attacker's instruction filled in (\texttt{yes}) or left empty
(\texttt{no}). The only difference inside a pair is the observation. 4,250
examples, 1,598 pairs, \texttt{policy\_conflict} from five generic policies.
Derivations: \texttt{docs/external-\{agentdojo,injecagent\}.md}.

\textbf{Policy checking transfers, and stays identity-dependent.} 2B v4
scores 0.849 AUPRC on AgentDojo \texttt{policy\_conflict} against a 0.237
base rate and a rule baseline at base rate, on policies, a tool schema, and
traces it never saw. Eval-time stripping drops it to 0.694 and the
train-stripped model to 0.390: the identity dependence of \S\ref{sec:identity}
on external data. On InjecAgent it transfers again (0.965, 0.977 against
0.464); stripping barely moves it there because the five policies are global
rules about the call, not about what the principal named. The 2B/8B ordering
on this dimension is not robust (8B below 2B on v4, above on v6); both sizes
rank violations well (AUROC 0.91--0.94) and differ in how many benign calls
they place above them.

\textbf{Injection at v4: 2B fails, 8B transfers, neither reads the untrusted
text.} 2B v4 is below base rate on AgentDojo injection (0.128, AUROC 0.348):
its injection head never fires on this domain (raw logits for all 4,133 calls
between $-5.0$ and $-3.0$) while its policy head separates cleanly on the same
rows. 8B v4 reaches 0.701 (AUROC 0.895). But on InjecAgent, with the call
held fixed, the 8B v4 injection score rises on only 35\% of pairs and is
unchanged on 39\%, and 2B v4 is at coin-flip; both heads sit at raw logit
$-4$ on poisoned and clean alike. What transferred at 8B v4 was call-level,
whether the action is something the user asked for, not a reading of the
injected instruction. The untrained 8B agent recognises the same thing: it
flags every InjecAgent attacker call as \textsc{stop}
(\texttt{unauthorized\_scope} 1.000) and is at chance on injection.

\textbf{The cause was a label leak in our generator; removing it is
sufficient.} Contrasting rendered calls between injected and non-injected
synthetic examples showed that the v1--v5 generator wrote an
\texttt{instructed\_target} argument into every injected call and no other
(presence 1.00 vs.\ 0.00 over 13,312 v4 rows) and drew the target from five
literals, three containing ``attacker'' (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-leak}).
The training signal was carried by an argument key; the observation never had
to be read. v6 removes the key, fills the target argument for benign calls
too, and renders 300--2,000 character multi-format observations with the
instruction at a random position. Retrained on v6 with identical size and
recipe, 2B reaches 0.693 AUPRC (AUROC 0.928) on AgentDojo injection and 8B
0.838 (0.966), and both heads fire (2B v6 median raw logit $+4.5$ for
positives vs.\ $-3.0$). On InjecAgent both rank the poisoned variant above
its clean twin on 97.7\% of pairs, reaching 0.878 and 0.943 AUPRC on a label
whose only evidence is in the untrusted text; stripping identity from 8B v6
leaves injection and pair win unchanged (0.953, 0.978), so the reading is
content-driven. Meanwhile the synthetic injection AUPRC did not change (0.802
to 0.788 for 2B, 0.791 to 0.795 for 8B). The leak had not inflated the
synthetic score; it had determined what the head learned to read, and no
synthetic split could have shown it.

\textbf{What does not transfer.} \texttt{unauthorized\_scope} is at base rate
on AgentDojo for every checker (0.21--0.36 vs.\ 0.253); the label counts
harmless auxiliary reads as out of scope. On InjecAgent, 8B v4 has signal
(0.849 vs.\ 0.752 base) and 8B v6 does not (0.677) unless the port's
placeholder principal and scope fields are removed (0.917): the v6 head is
misled by identity fields the port filled generically. Calibration does not
transfer: macro ECE is 0.20--0.27 on AgentDojo against under 0.03 on
synthetic data. Refitting isotonic calibrators on half of the AgentDojo calls
brings the other half to macro ECE 0.008 (2B) and 0.010 (8B) with AUPRC
unchanged, most of the gain by 250--500 labelled calls. A deployment needs a
few hundred in-domain labels for its probabilities, not for its ranking.
Over all four AgentDojo suites (30,143 calls), the 8B v4 injection transfer
holds on banking, workspace, and slack (AUPRC 0.70, 0.75, 0.43 at base rates
0.15, 0.08, 0.19) and is weak on travel (0.13 at 0.025); 2B v4 is at base
rate everywhere, and policy labels outside banking are one generic policy per
suite and too weak to claim transfer on (Table~\ref{tab:agentdojo-suites}).
