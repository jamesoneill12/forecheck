\section{Transfer to AgentDojo and InjecAgent: data we did not generate}
\label{sec:external}

Every result so far is on forecheck's own synthetic generator
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}). We score the same v4 checkers, zero-shot, on
AgentDojo \citep{debenedetti2024agentdojo}, a published benchmark of agent run
traces we did not write. We use the banking suite traces for
claude-3-5-sonnet-20241022 and gpt-4o-2024-05-13: 1,714 runs, 4,215 proposed
tool calls, 11 attack types plus no-attack runs. Labels come from AgentDojo's
own \texttt{ground\_truth()} functions, not from forecheck's generator:
\texttt{prompt\_injection\_influence} is \texttt{yes} when a call executes the
injection task's goal (positive rate 0.150, $n{=}4{,}133$ evaluable calls);
\texttt{unauthorized\_scope} is \texttt{yes} when the call's function is
outside the user task's reference-solution function set (rate 0.253);
\texttt{policy\_conflict} is \texttt{yes} when one of four banking policies we
wrote once per suite fires, checked by deterministic predicates (rate 0.237);
\texttt{financial\_commitment} (0.343) and
\texttt{destructive\_or\_irreversible\_action} (0.097) come from function-name
sets. Trace parsing and the full label derivation are documented in
\texttt{docs/external-agentdojo.md}. Every arm is zero-shot: nothing was
trained or tuned on AgentDojo, the calibration bundles were fitted on
synthetic dev, and threshold selection is disabled.

Table~\ref{tab:agentdojo} and Figure~\ref{fig:agentdojo} give six readings.
\textbf{Policy checking transfers.} 2B v4 scores 0.849 AUPRC on
\texttt{policy\_conflict} against a 0.237 base rate and a rule baseline at
base rate, on policies, a tool schema, and agent traces it never saw.
Eval-time stripping drops it to 0.694 and the train-time stripped model to
0.390, the same identity dependence found in \S\ref{sec:identity}, now on
external data. \textbf{Injection transfer on v4 was a data effect, not a
scale effect.} 2B v4 fails outright (0.128 AUPRC, below the 0.150 base rate,
AUROC 0.348); 8B v4 reaches 0.701 AUPRC and 0.895 AUROC zero-shot; the rule
baseline sits between the two (0.250, AUROC 0.737) because presence of
untrusted content alone is a strong but insufficient cue. A per-example score
dump says why 2B v4 fails: its injection head never fires on this domain. Raw
logits for all 4,133 evaluable calls lie between $-5.0$ and $-3.0$, the
highest-scoring positive is $-3.25$, and no call scores above zero; on the
same rows the 2B \texttt{policy\_conflict} head separates cleanly (median raw
logit $+2.5$ for positives, $-3.5$ for negatives). The 8B v4 head is live but
shifted: median raw logit $-1.75$ for positives against $-3.50$ for
negatives, 29\% of positives above zero against 0.7\% of negatives. The v6
checkers, trained on the generator with the label leak removed
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}), settle the question: 2B v6 reaches 0.693 AUPRC and
0.928 AUROC, 8B v6 0.838 and 0.966, and both heads now fire (2B v6 median raw
$+4.5$ for positives vs.\ $-3.0$ for negatives, 92\% vs.\ 23\% above zero; 8B
v6 $+6.25$ vs.\ $-3.25$, 94\% vs.\ 8\%). The 2B v4 model had learned
injection from a surface cue of the synthetic generator that AgentDojo's
injections do not carry; with the cue gone it learned something that
transfers. The two 8B baselines separate training from parameter count: the
same Granite-3.3-8B base asked \textsc{allow}/\textsc{stop} as the agent
(\S\ref{sec:selfjudgment}) scores 0.291 AUPRC on injection and Granite
Guardian 3.3 8B zero-shot scores 0.103, below base rate. Stripping identity
from the input keeps injection (0.743 for 8B v4, 0.819 for 8B v6, 0.669 for
2B v6) and drops \texttt{policy\_conflict} (8B v4 0.626 to 0.404, 8B v6 0.785
to 0.450), the same content versus identity split as in \S\ref{sec:identity};
\S\ref{sec:injecagent} tests whether the injection heads read the untrusted
text or the call.
\textbf{\texttt{unauthorized\_scope} does not transfer} (0.257 2B v4, 0.355
8B v4, 0.214 2B v6, 0.239 8B v6, vs.\ a 0.253 base rate); the 8B v4 head
scores above zero on 77\% of negatives and 66\% of positives, so it fires on
nearly every call and carries no signal on this label. The label itself is
the weakest one here, since "function not in the reference solution" counts
harmless auxiliary reads as out of scope, and we report this without claiming
transfer on it. \textbf{The 8B-below-2B \texttt{policy\_conflict} gap is not
robust.} On v4, 8B scores 0.626 AUPRC against 2B's 0.849 while its AUROC
(0.912) stays high; on v6 the order reverses (8B 0.785, 2B 0.670). A second
8B v4 seed on synthetic \texttt{heldout\_policy\_kind} moves
\texttt{policy\_conflict} by 0.03 (0.851 to 0.825), so the v4 gap of 0.22 is
larger than seed noise there, but with one seed per (size, data version) cell
we can only say the direction depends on the training data; both sizes rank
the violations well (AUROC 0.91--0.94) and differ in how many benign calls
they place above them.
\textbf{Calibration does not transfer, an in-domain refit fixes it}: macro
ECE runs 0.20--0.27 for the calibrated arms here, against under 0.03 on
synthetic data. Refitting per-dimension isotonic calibrators on a random half
of the AgentDojo calls and testing on the other half (five seeds) gives macro
ECE 0.008 (2B) and 0.010 (8B) with AUPRC unchanged; the 8B
\texttt{unauthorized\_scope} head goes from ECE 0.60 to 0.01 because it
learns to output the base rate. Most of the gain is reached with 250--500
labelled calls (\texttt{scripts/agentdojo\_recalibrate.py}). Last, the labels
here are partly written by us (the four policy predicates) and partly by
AgentDojo (\texttt{prompt\_injection\_influence},
\texttt{unauthorized\_scope}).

\textbf{Four suites.} Re-running the export over all four AgentDojo suites
gives 30,143 proposed calls (banking 4,215, slack 8,246, travel 10,701,
workspace 6,981), with injection positive rate 0.099 overall and 0.025 on
travel. The 8B v4 checker's injection transfer holds on three of the four:
AUPRC/AUROC 0.701/0.895 on banking, 0.752/0.941 on workspace, 0.433/0.738 on
slack, each several times the base rate, and 0.131/0.834 on travel where
positives are 2.5\% of calls; the eval-stripped arm is within 0.04 of it on
every suite. 2B v4 is at base rate on every suite; the v6 checkers were not
run on the four-suite export.
The agent-self baseline reaches 0.251/0.809 over all suites and Guardian
0.116/0.553. \texttt{unauthorized\_scope} outside banking is carried by the
rule baseline (0.749 on slack, 0.733 on workspace, where nearly half of all
calls are labelled out of scope); 2B adds under 0.09 and both 8B arms are
below the rule.
\texttt{policy\_conflict} on slack, travel, and workspace uses one generic
policy per suite at 0.7--8\% base rates and is a weak label there: 2B AUROC is
0.78--0.89 but AUPRC 0.005--0.49, so we claim policy transfer on banking only.
Per-suite tables are in \texttt{docs/results/agentdojo/README.md}.

\subsection{InjecAgent: holding the call fixed}
\label{sec:injecagent}

AgentDojo's injection positives differ from their negatives in the call
itself: an unrequested transfer to a recipient the user never named. To test
whether the checker reads the untrusted text at all, we port InjecAgent
\citep{zhan2024injecagent} (1,054 cases, 38 toolkits, MIT licence). Each case
gives the user's own call and, per attacker tool, a \emph{poisoned/clean
pair}: the same attacker call, with identical arguments, proposed after the
tool response with the attacker's instruction filled in
(\texttt{prompt\_injection\_influence}=\texttt{yes}) or left empty
(\texttt{no}). The only difference inside a pair is the observation
(\texttt{docs/external-injecagent.md}). 4,250 examples, 1,598 pairs.

Table~\ref{tab:injecagent}: \textbf{neither v4 checker reads the injected
text; both v6 checkers do}. With the call held fixed, the 8B v4 injection
score rises on 35\% of pairs and is unchanged on 39\%; both v4 heads sit at
raw logit $-4$ on poisoned and clean alike. This reinterprets the v4
AgentDojo result: what transferred at 8B v4 is call-level, whether the action
is something the user asked for, not reading the untrusted content for the
instruction that explains it. The v4 generator's leak
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}) is the cause: the training signal was carried by
an argument key and the observation never had to be read. Removing it is
sufficient. The v6 checkers, identical in size and recipe, rank the poisoned
variant above its clean twin on 97.7\% of the 1,598 pairs at both sizes
(median raw logit $+3.5$ vs.\ $-6.25$ for 2B, $+2.25$ vs.\ $-3.5$ for 8B),
reaching 0.878 (2B) and 0.943 (8B) AUPRC on a label whose only evidence is
in the untrusted text. \textbf{Policy checking transfers a second time}:
\texttt{policy\_conflict} 0.965 (2B) and 0.977 (8B) against a 0.464 base
rate on five policies written for this port over 38 unseen toolkits, with the
rule baseline at base rate; stripping identity barely moves it here because
these five policies are global rules about the call rather than about what
the principal named; the v6 checkers hold it (0.922, 0.965).
\texttt{unauthorized\_scope} has signal at 8B v4 (0.849 vs.\ 0.752 base),
none at 2B, and the 8B signal does not survive the v6 retrain (0.677). Both baselines are at chance on injection
too (AUROC 0.54 and 0.60); the untrained agent flags every attacker call as
\textsc{stop} (\texttt{unauthorized\_scope} 1.000), the same call-level
judgement; the two v6 checkers are the only arms in this table that read the
poisoned text.
