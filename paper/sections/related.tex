\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{LLM guardrails.} Llama Guard \citep{inan2023llamaguard}, Granite
Guardian \citep{padhi2024graniteguardian}, ShieldGemma
\citep{zeng2024shieldgemma}, NVIDIA Aegis \citep{ghosh2024aegis}, and
gpt-oss-safeguard \citep{openai2025gptosssafeguard} classify content/harm
over a conversation turn or action; Agent Action Guard
\citep{agentactionguard2025} is an embedding MLP over tool name and arguments
with no identity or policy input. We do not claim they fail at their intended
purpose; \S\ref{sec:identity} shows they are at chance specifically on
delegated-authority dimensions they were not trained to answer.

\textbf{Agent safety benchmarks.} AgentHarm
\citep{andriushchenko2024agentharm}, ToolEmu \citep{ruan2023toolemu},
InjecAgent \citep{zhan2024injecagent}, and AgentDojo
\citep{debenedetti2024agentdojo} measure end-to-end agent behavior or
injection success with an outcome judge. They answer ``did the agent do
something bad,'' not ``should this action have been allowed given who it was
for,'' and emit no per-action calibrated probability a policy engine can
compose. We reuse two as external test sets by deriving per-action labels
from their own ground-truth functions.

\textbf{Authorization engines and declared intent.} OPA \citep{opa}, Cedar
\citep{cedar}, and XACML \citep{xacml} decide allow/deny symbolically from a
fully specified request and policy. OWASP's agentic guidance
\citep{owasp2025agentic} and positions built on it \citep{capiscio2026intent}
hold that authorization must be deterministic and auditable: the agent
declares a typed mandate and an enforcement point checks each call against
it. Our results support the premise (the agent is at chance on its own scope)
and show the limit: once the mandate is natural-language policy, the symbolic
checker (our rule baseline) drops to 0.33--0.50 AUPRC on
\texttt{policy\_conflict} and \texttt{unauthorized\_scope}, and only a learned
reader recovers it across paraphrases. forecheck keeps the decision
deterministic and puts the learned component where that guidance allows it:
the scorer never authorises, it prices the review. Temperature scaling
\citep{platt1999probabilistic,guo2017calibration} and the Wilson interval
\citep{wilson1927probable} are standard; we apply them to policy
\emph{composition}, where \S\ref{sec:composition}'s $k$-invariance is a
function of calibration sharpness.
