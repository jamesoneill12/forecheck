\section{Problem setting}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Contract.} forecheck scores a proposed tool call against an
\texttt{ActionContext}: the principal's entitlements, the scope delegated to
the agent (a possibly strict subset), a trust-labelled trajectory of prior
observations, the applicable policy clause(s), and the proposed action (tool,
arguments, destination), all as typed fields. It
returns one probability per risk dimension. A separate policy engine turns
the vector into \textsc{allow}/\textsc{review}/\textsc{deny} under a
customer-authored bundle; the checker itself never authorises.

\textbf{Threat model.} The adversary controls tool outputs and retrieved
documents, which the checker renders verbatim inside tag-escaped trajectory
blocks so untrusted text cannot impersonate a trusted field. The adversary
does not control the entitlement records or the policy store; the thesis
depends on that assumption. The checker is itself an injection target, and
adaptive attacks against it are not evaluated here.

\textbf{Eleven risk dimensions.} Six are decidable from the rendered call
alone once the renderer includes the deciding field:
\texttt{destructive\_or\_irreversible\_action}, \texttt{financial\_commitment},
\texttt{external\_communication}, \texttt{untrusted\_destination},
\texttt{sensitive\_data\_exposure}, and, from generator version v4,
\texttt{privilege\_escalation}, whose authority-before/after fields are
written into the call text. Two are trajectory dimensions
(\texttt{prompt\_injection\_influence}, \texttt{suspicious\_action\_sequence}).
Three are functions of the identity and policy fields:
\texttt{unauthorized\_scope} (is the required scope inside the principal's
entitlements \emph{and} the delegated scope?), \texttt{policy\_conflict}
(does the call violate a stated clause?), and \texttt{insufficient\_context}
(was enough delegated information present to decide?). Whether a reader of
the text can recover each label is tested in \S\ref{sec:labelvalidity}.

\textbf{Data.} A template generator samples a latent scenario, derives the
eleven labels deterministically from it, and renders a context.
Table~\ref{tab:versions} lists the versions used; where a comparison depends
on their differences we say so. Known defects beyond
the two the generator was revised for: a 5\% chance of marking any resource
irreversible, including reads; a 5\% chance of a financial amount on a
non-financial tool; and a background destination on 15--35\% of non-sending
calls, which drives the \texttt{untrusted\_destination} and
\texttt{sensitive\_data\_exposure} labels without appearing in the call.
Splits are \texttt{train}, \texttt{calibration}, \texttt{dev}, \texttt{test},
\texttt{heldout\_family} (a stratified subset of tools withheld), and the two
policy splits of \S\ref{sec:generalisation}; \texttt{heldout\_family} scores
within 0.01 of \texttt{test} for every arm. Every synthetic number measures
whether an arm learned the generator's label function.

\textbf{Arms.} A LoRA-adapted decoder \citep{hu2021lora} at 2B
(\texttt{granite-3.3-2b-instruct}) and 8B, scored by candidate-token logits
per dimension and temperature-scaled on the calibration split
\citep{guo2017calibration}; two linear-head encoders (ModernBERT-large
\citep{warner2024modernbert}, granite-embedding-r2); a deterministic
\emph{rule baseline} of field lookups, whose \texttt{policy\_conflict} rule is
a constant and whose \texttt{unauthorized\_scope} rule does not intersect
entitlements with delegated scope, so on those two dimensions it is an
uninformative floor at the positive rate, not a symbolic policy engine; two
zero-shot safety models given the same policy text as a custom criterion,
Granite Guardian 3.3 8B \citep{padhi2024graniteguardian} and
gpt-oss-safeguard 20B \citep{openai2025gptosssafeguard}, the latter emitting a
binary verdict; and an \emph{agent self-judgment} arm in which the untrained
8B instruct model \citep{ibm2024granite3} receives the same context in an
agent system prompt and returns one \textsc{allow}/\textsc{stop} verdict.

\textbf{Metrics.} We report AUPRC, whose chance level is the positive rate,
with per-dimension evaluable $n$ and positive rate (287 to 6,653 rows; 0.07
to 0.52). Seven dimensions are above 0.99 for the trained checker and four are
solved by the rule baseline, so we also report a \emph{non-saturated macro}
over the seven dimensions on which the rule baseline is below 0.9. Synthetic estimates are single-seed unless stated;
external cells with saved per-example scores carry 95\% bootstrap intervals.
