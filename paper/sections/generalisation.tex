\section{Policy generalization: phrasing vs.\ kind}
\label{sec:generalisation}

Robustness to unseen \emph{tools} is not robustness to unseen
\emph{policies}, and the latter is the deployment test: customers write their
own clauses. \texttt{heldout\_policy\_kind} withholds entire predicate kinds
(e.g.\ \texttt{forbid\_recipient\_domain}) from training;
\texttt{heldout\_policy\_phrasing} withholds one paraphrase of every kind the
model did train on. v4 has 15 kinds with 2 withheld; v5 has 30 with 4.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\small

for v5; 8B: two seeds); other cells are seed 0. Rule baseline on that split is
0.325. Macro AUPRC and per-seed values in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-generalisation}.}
\label{tab:policy-generalisation}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule

\midrule
test & 0.994 & 0.996 & 0.990 \\

heldout\_policy\_kind & \textbf{0.717} & \textbf{0.725} & \textbf{0.838} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\textbf{Unseen phrasing generalizes; unseen kinds do not, even with twice as
many kinds.} A paraphrase never seen in training leaves
\texttt{policy\_conflict} at 0.98--0.99 AUPRC, matching the test split: the
model reads the clause's meaning, not a template id. On withheld kinds 2B
lands at 0.717 (v4) and 0.725 (v5) across three seeds each; doubling the kind
inventory moves the mean by less than one seed standard deviation. The model
is far above the 0.325 rule baseline, so it has learned something about
unseen kinds, but not the general operation of checking a call against an
arbitrary clause. \textbf{Capacity helps partially.} 8B reaches 0.838 (two
seeds: 0.851, 0.825), a real gain over the 2B band and still well short of the
0.98 phrasing ceiling. The supportable claim is ``one model, policies of a
kind seen in training, any phrasing,'' not ``arbitrary new policy kinds.''
