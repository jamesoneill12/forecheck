\section{Policy generalization: kind vs.\ phrasing}
\label{sec:generalisation}

Robustness to unseen \emph{tools} (\texttt{heldout\_family}) is not
robustness to unseen \emph{policy kinds} or \emph{phrasings}, and the latter
is the more product-relevant test: a deployment needs the classifier to
apply a customer's own wording, not just handle a new tool under a
training-set policy. ADR 0010 adds two splits: \texttt{heldout\_policy\_kind}
withholds entire predicate kinds (e.g.\ \texttt{forbid\_recipient\_domain})
from training; \texttt{heldout\_policy\_phrasing} withholds one paraphrase
index of every kind the model \emph{did} train on. \texttt{v4} additionally
fixes a renderer defect that left \texttt{privilege\_escalation}
undecidable from text (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

Figure~\ref{fig:policy-generalisation} makes the kind-vs-phrasing asymmetry
visible: bars for test, \texttt{heldout\_family}, and
\texttt{heldout\_policy\_phrasing} sit close together near the ceiling, while
\texttt{heldout\_policy\_kind} drops toward, though still above, the rule
baseline line, for every generator and both model sizes.

\textbf{Unseen phrasing generalizes.} A paraphrase index never seen in
training leaves \texttt{policy\_conflict} at 0.980--0.993 AUPRC, matching the
in-distribution test split (0.990--0.996): the model reads the clause's
meaning, not a template id. \textbf{Unseen policy kinds do not, even with
twice as many kinds.} ADR 0011's v5 triples the kind inventory (15 to 30)
while withholding the same proportion (4 of 30 vs.\ 2 of 15). Across three
training seeds, \texttt{policy\_conflict} on \texttt{heldout\_policy\_kind}
lands at 0.704, 0.755, and 0.717 AUPRC (mean 0.725, sample sd 0.026,
$n{=}3$) -- essentially unchanged from the v4 15-kind recipe's own
three-seed band of 0.752, 0.689, and 0.711 (mean 0.717, sample sd 0.032,
$n{=}3$; \S\ref{sec:limitations}). The v5 mean sits within one sample sd of
the v4 mean, so tripling the kind inventory is not a real improvement, it is
inside the seed noise the 15-kind recipe already showed. The rule baseline
on this split is 0.325, so the model is still far above chance; it has just
not learned the general operation. \textbf{Model size helps, partially.} An
8B LoRA on the unchanged v4 data reaches 0.851 AUPRC (AUROC 0.916) on the
same \texttt{heldout\_policy\_kind} \texttt{policy\_conflict} cell -- a real
gain over both 2B bands -- but still well short of the $\sim$0.98
phrasing-generalization ceiling both sizes reach. Capacity recovers some of
the missing generalization; it does not close the gap.

The v4 fix also confirms \texttt{privilege\_escalation}'s earlier
AUROC-high/AUPRC-low signature (\S\ref{sec:limitations}) was a data defect,
not a model limit: rendering the deciding
\texttt{authority\_before}/\texttt{authority\_after} values lifts it from
0.505 to 1.000 AUPRC on \texttt{heldout\_family}.
