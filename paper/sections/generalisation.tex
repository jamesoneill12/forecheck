\section{Policy generalisation: phrasing vs.\ kind}
\label{sec:generalisation}

Robustness to unseen \emph{tools} is not robustness to unseen
\emph{policies}, and the latter is the deployment test: customers write
their own clauses. \texttt{heldout\_policy\_kind} withholds entire predicate
kinds (e.g.\ \texttt{forbid\_recipient\_domain}) from training;
\texttt{heldout\_policy\_phrasing} withholds one paraphrase of every kind the
model did train on. v4 has 15 kinds with 2 withheld; v5 has 30 with 4.

\quad rule baseline (= pos.\ rate) & 0.405 & 0.325 & 0.405 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{\texttt{policy\_conflict} AUPRC across splits. Kind-split cells are means over training seeds (2B: three seeds, sd 0.032 for v4 and 0.026 for v5; 8B: two seeds, 0.851 and 0.825); other cells are seed 0. The rule baseline's policy rule is a constant, so it scores the positive rate. Macro AUPRC and per-seed values in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-generalisation}.}
\label{tab:policy-generalisation}

\textbf{Unseen phrasing generalises; unseen kinds do not.} A paraphrase never
seen in training leaves \texttt{policy\_conflict} at 0.98--0.99, matching the
test split: the model reads the clause's meaning, not a template id. On
withheld kinds 2B lands at 0.717 (v4) and 0.725 (v5), well above the
positive-rate floor but far from the phrasing ceiling. 8B reaches 0.838 on
two seeds. The supportable claim is ``one model, policies of a kind seen in
training, any phrasing,'' not ``arbitrary new policy kinds.''

\textbf{What the kind split can and cannot say.} The v4 and v5 kind cells are
means over training seeds on \emph{different} withheld kinds (2 vs.\ 4), so
their near-equality is not evidence that a larger inventory fails to help:
the dominant variance component, which kinds were withheld, is not estimated,
and with three seeds at sd 0.03 the minimum detectable difference is about
0.08. We therefore report the result as ``no evidence of an inventory
effect at this size,'' not as its absence.
