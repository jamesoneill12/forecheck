\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Synthetic data with enumerated defects.} Every number outside
\S\ref{sec:external} is on data from our own generator and measures whether
an arm learned the generator's label function. No human-labelled or
production-traffic evaluation exists and no real-world safety claim is made.
Beyond the two defects the generator was revised for, it marks 5\% of
resources irreversible at random including on reads, stamps a financial
amount on 5\% of non-financial tools, and attaches a background destination
to 15--35\% of non-sending calls; \texttt{insufficient\_context} is not
recoverable from the text by a blind reader, and \texttt{sensitive\_data\_exposure}
depends on a tenancy fact the rendering does not show. Every number on those
two dimensions, and every 11-dimension macro that includes them, carries that
caveat. Generator seed nondeterminism was found and fixed mid-project; v2/v3
data are not byte-reproducible from their configs.

\textbf{The injection leak contaminates more than \S\ref{sec:external}.}
Every synthetic injection number before v6, the judge's $\kappa{=}0.905$ on
that dimension, and the agent-self injection reversal in
\S\ref{sec:identity} were measured on text carrying the leaked argument.
We mark them and report leak-excluded macros, but the judge and the
agent-self arm have not been re-run on v6 text. The v6 fix changes
observation length and format as well as removing the key; the two are not
separated.

\textbf{The InjecAgent pair test's confounds are controlled, not eliminated
in general.} The clean twin is shorter than the poisoned one in every pair,
and length- and destination-trust-matched controls confirm the v6 win is not
either (\S\ref{sec:external}); both controls are themselves synthetic filler
text, not real benign tool output, so a checker specialised to distinguish
our filler from genuine content would not be caught by them.

\textbf{Uncertainty.} Synthetic results are single-seed point estimates
except where stated; only the \texttt{policy\_conflict} kind-split cell has
three 2B seeds (sd 0.03) and two 8B seeds, both v6 checkers are single-seed,
and the 8B v4 two-seed gap (test macro 0.956 vs.\ 0.948) falls on two
dimensions, not one. Bootstrap intervals are given only where per-example
scores were saved (InjecAgent). The 8B v4 AgentDojo injection cell moved by
0.1 between runs we could not reconcile. Differences smaller than these
bands are interpreted nowhere in the paper by intent; where we have failed,
the reader should discount them. Kind-split variance over \emph{which} kinds
were withheld is not estimated.

\textbf{Baselines.} The rule baseline is an uninformative floor on
\texttt{policy\_conflict} and \texttt{unauthorized\_scope}. The symbolic
baseline was run on splits regenerated with the current generator, not the
exact v4 files the decoders were scored on, so its cells and the decoders'
are on different draws with the same seed and config. The zero-shot
guardrails were prompted through chat-template arguments verified only
against test doubles, and that is how the Granite Guardian verdict-position
error survived until this revision; an audit found it, and every Guardian
number in this paper is from the corrected read. No positive control on a
native risk category has been run for either model, though the corrected
Guardian reaches AUROC 0.98 on \texttt{financial\_commitment} and 0.999
AUPRC on AgentDojo's financial label, so the read is live. gpt-oss-safeguard emits a binary verdict and is scored on 400
rows. The agent-self arm answers one question and is scored on eleven.
Baseline rows on the external benchmarks use 1,000-row subsamples with
different positive rates from the checker rows.

\textbf{External labels are partly ours.} AgentDojo's policy predicates and
InjecAgent's five policies are self-written; injection and scope labels are
the benchmarks' own. InjecAgent's policy label is a call-shape judgement and
does not bear on identity. AgentDojo's \texttt{unauthorized\_scope} counts
harmless auxiliary reads as out of scope and has not been adjudicated. The
InjecAgent port fills principal and scope with placeholders. The v6 checkers
have not been run on the four-suite AgentDojo export. Calibration does not
transfer without a few hundred in-domain labels.

\textbf{Composition and approval.} \S\ref{sec:composition} is on v2 data and
the v2 decoder; the approval-elimination curve's non-zero values exist only
after reweighting a 58\%-incident split to an asserted 5\% deployment rate,
and its denominator is all scored actions, not the review queue
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-approval}). No latency, cost, fail-open
behaviour, or adaptive-attack evaluation of the checker is reported. A
single English template generator produces every synthetic example.
