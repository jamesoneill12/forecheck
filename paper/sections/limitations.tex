\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Synthetic data only.} Every number here is on data from forecheck's
own scenario generator (\texttt{docs/evaluation-plan.md}, classes 1--2). No
human-labelled or production-traffic evaluation exists at submission time,
and no real-world safety claim is made: every result measures whether an
arm learned the generator's own label function.

\textbf{Independent labels exist for label validity, not for real-world
risk.} \S\ref{sec:labelvalidity} adds a blind LLM judge as an independent
reader of the same rendered text, which is a check on whether a label is
observable in the text at all, not a check against real-world ground truth
-- the judge never saw production traffic either. That check is reassuring
on \texttt{policy\_conflict} ($\kappa{=}0.71$) and directionally consistent
on \texttt{unauthorized\_scope} ($\kappa{=}0.39$, differing definitions), but
finds \texttt{insufficient\_context} ($\kappa{=}0.06$) unrecoverable from the
text by an independent reader. Every \texttt{insufficient\_context} number
in this paper should be read with that caveat attached until the generator's
rendering makes the context gap explicit.

\textbf{Generator regularities.} The model may be exploiting template
regularities in how the generator writes policy clauses and entitlement
lists, rather than a general policy-reading ability. The
\texttt{heldout\_policy\_kind}/\texttt{heldout\_policy\_phrasing} splits
(\S\ref{sec:generalisation}) probe this and only partly rule it out:
phrasing generalizes cleanly, kind does not, consistent with a lookup over
kinds rather than the underlying operation. The encoder's earlier null
result on both splits is uninformative the same way -- it never learned
\texttt{policy\_conflict} at all, so a flat curve shows nothing.

\textbf{\texttt{privilege\_escalation} render defect (v2/v3).} The
\texttt{authority\_before}/\texttt{authority\_after} fields deciding this
label were never rendered into the tool-call text in v2/v3, so every arm's
0.46--0.53 AUPRC there reflects a data defect: 78 of 79 dev positives were
textually indistinguishable from an in-scope re-grant. Fixed in v4
(\S\ref{sec:generalisation}); numbers sourced from v2/v3 elsewhere should be
read with this in mind.

\textbf{\texttt{prompt\_injection\_influence} label leak (v1--v5).} After the
2B checker failed on AgentDojo injection (\S\ref{sec:external}) we contrasted
the rendered call between injected and non-injected synthetic examples. The
generator wrote an \texttt{instructed\_target} argument into every injected
call and into no other call (presence 1.00 vs.\ 0.00 over 13,312 v4 rows), put
a \texttt{to} argument into 100\% of injected calls vs.\ 3\% of others, and
drew the injected target from five literals, three containing the word
``attacker''. A key-presence detector would score 1.0 AUPRC on this
dimension, above every trained arm's $\sim$0.80, so the synthetic injection
numbers in this paper (Tables \ref{tab:identity-ablation},
\ref{tab:model-vs-judge}) and the judge's $\kappa{=}0.905$ on it are not
evidence of injection detection; the judge saw the same argument. The identity dimensions do not use the leaked keys and are
unaffected. The AgentDojo and InjecAgent results are the first leak-free
measurements of the v4 checkers: 2B fails (0.128 AUPRC), 8B partly transfers
(0.701). The generator is fixed in v6 (renderer 2.0.0: no
\texttt{instructed\_target}, target argument key chosen from the tool kind
and filled for benign calls too, shared target pools, 300--2,000 character
multi-format observations) with a leakage check on argument-key presence
parity. Retrained on v6, the synthetic injection AUPRC is unchanged (0.802 to
0.788 for 2B, 0.791 to 0.795 for 8B) while the AgentDojo injection AUPRC
rises to 0.693 (2B) and 0.838 (8B) and the InjecAgent poisoned-over-clean
pair win rises from 0.504 (2B) and 0.353 (8B) to 0.977 at both sizes
(\S\ref{sec:external}): the leak did not inflate the synthetic score, it
changed what the head learned to read
(\texttt{docs/results/notes/injection-label-leak-diagnosis.md}).
Every other synthetic dimension is within 0.01 of v4 except
\texttt{policy\_conflict} on \texttt{heldout\_policy\_kind}, which drops to
0.692 (2B) and 0.767 (8B), inside the seed band below for 2B and 0.08 below
the single v4 seed for 8B.

\textbf{Seed variance (three seeds each for v4 and v5).} Three training
seeds for decoder v4 agree on macro AUPRC to within 0.001 on every split
(e.g.\ 0.960, 0.959, 0.960 on test). But the \texttt{heldout\_policy\_kind}
\texttt{policy\_conflict} cell moves across a 0.06 range between seeds
(0.752, 0.689, 0.711; mean 0.717, sample sd 0.032) -- an order of magnitude
more than the macro noise floor -- so differences smaller than that on this
specific cell are not distinguishable from seed noise. Three seeds of the
v5 (30-kind) recipe show the same range (0.704, 0.755, 0.717; mean 0.725,
sample sd 0.026), which is what lets \S\ref{sec:generalisation} compare the
two bands directly instead of a single point each. The 8B numbers are from
one seed except where noted; a second 8B seed reaches macro AUPRC 0.948 on
test against 0.956 for the first, with the whole gap on
\texttt{insufficient\_context} (0.682 vs.\ 0.767) and the other ten
dimensions within 0.003, so 8B-vs-2B gaps on that dimension are inside seed
noise. On \texttt{heldout\_policy\_kind} the second seed puts
\texttt{policy\_conflict} at 0.825 against 0.851, inside the 2B seed band
above.

\textbf{Held-out-policy-kind failure (now tested at 30 kinds and 8B).}
\texttt{policy\_conflict} AUPRC drops from $\sim$0.98--0.99
(in-distribution, unseen-phrasing) to 0.65--0.76 on entirely unseen kinds at
2B. The v5 experiment (ADR 0011, 30 kinds, 4 withheld) has landed across
three seeds and is negative: mean 0.725, inside one sample sd of v4's mean
0.717 -- tripling the kind inventory did not fix this at 2B. An 8B LoRA on
the unchanged v4 data reaches 0.851, so capacity helps partially but does
not solve it. The supportable claim today is "one model, policies of a kind
seen in training (or with enough capacity to partially generalize), any
phrasing" -- not "arbitrary new policy kinds."

\textbf{External labels are partly self-written and uneven across suites.}
The AgentDojo labels (\S\ref{sec:external}) are partly written by us (the
policy predicates) and partly by AgentDojo
(\texttt{prompt\_injection\_influence}, \texttt{unauthorized\_scope}). The
banking suite has four tool-specific policies; slack, travel, and workspace
have one generic policy each, so the policy-transfer claim rests on banking
alone, and the four-suite injection result is one training run (8B v4, with
and without identity) on a label whose positive rate is 2.5\% on travel.
Calibration did not transfer: macro ECE runs 0.20--0.27 on AgentDojo against
under 0.03 on synthetic data, so only the ranking generalizes to this external
distribution. Refitting isotonic calibrators on half of the AgentDojo calls
brings macro ECE on the other half to 0.008 (2B) and 0.010 (8B) with AUPRC
unchanged, and most of the gain arrives by 250--500 labelled calls
(\texttt{docs/results/agentdojo/recalibration.md}), so a deployment needs a
few hundred in-domain labels for its probabilities, not for its ranking.

\textbf{Other caveats.} A single generator family (English, template
renderer) produces every example. The \S\ref{sec:composition} cost table is
the default severity tier, not tuned to any organization. Generator seed
nondeterminism was found and partially fixed mid-project
(\texttt{docs/results/notes/privilege-escalation-diagnosis.md}); v2/v3 data
are not byte-reproducible from their configs.
