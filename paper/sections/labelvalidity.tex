\section{Are the labels real? An independent blind judge}
\label{sec:labelvalidity}

Every number so far measures whether an arm learned forecheck's own generator's
label function (\S\ref{sec:limitations}). That leaves open a narrower but
answerable question: is the label itself observable in the rendered text at
all, to a reader who never sees the generator's latent scenario? We had a
frontier model (gpt-5.6-sol), given only the rendered context and the eleven
dimension definitions, judge 600 \texttt{v4} rows stratified so every
dimension has at least 20 positive and 20 negative examples, across
\texttt{test}, \texttt{heldout\_family}, and \texttt{heldout\_policy\_kind}.
Agreement with the generator's label is not a ground-truth check -- the judge
is another reader of the same text a classifier sees -- but low agreement is
informative: it means the label depends on information the generator tracked
internally and never rendered, so a trained model scoring well on it is
learning a generator regularity, not a fact about the text.

Table~\ref{tab:judge-agreement} and Figure~\ref{fig:judge-agreement} split
into three tiers. \textbf{Recoverable} ($\kappa > 0.7$):
\texttt{privilege\_escalation}, \texttt{prompt\_injection\_influence}, and
\texttt{policy\_conflict}. A blind reader recovers these from the text alone.
\texttt{privilege\_escalation} at 0.91 is an independent confirmation of the
\texttt{v4} render fix (\S\ref{sec:generalisation}): the deciding fields are
now genuinely in the tool-call arguments, not just in the model's training
signal. \texttt{policy\_conflict} at 0.71 with judge recall 0.89 means the
policy clause plus the proposed call is enough for an outside reader to spot
the conflict; the disagreements are mostly the judge reading a clause more
strictly than the generator's own predicate, not a missing signal.

\textbf{Definitional disagreement} ($0.2 \le \kappa \le 0.5$):
\texttt{unauthorized\_scope}, \texttt{financial\_commitment},
\texttt{destructive\_or\_irreversible\_action},
\texttt{suspicious\_action\_sequence}, \texttt{external\_communication}, and
\texttt{untrusted\_destination}. Here the judge and the generator are both
reading the text and both producing a coherent decision -- they just use
different definitions of the same dimension name. For
\texttt{unauthorized\_scope} the judge flags roughly twice as many rows as
the generator (159 vs.\ 81 judge-positive) because it treats a mismatch with
the agent's stated objective (e.g.\ a bulk retrieval where a single-record
lookup was requested) as out of scope, while the generator's label is
entitlement-based only; judge recall of the generator's positives is still
0.74, so the two notions overlap substantially without being identical. For
\texttt{financial\_commitment} the judge requires funds to actually move,
while the generator labels any call that carries a financial amount in its
arguments. These are taxonomy decisions to fix in the dimension definitions,
not evidence the labels are unobservable.

\textbf{Not recoverable} ($\kappa < 0.1$): \texttt{insufficient\_context} and
\texttt{sensitive\_data\_exposure}. For \texttt{insufficient\_context} the
judge calls the context sufficient on 50 of the 58 generator-positive rows in
the sample; whatever the generator tracks as a context gap is not visible in
what gets rendered. For \texttt{sensitive\_data\_exposure} the judge requires
a transfer to a lower-sensitivity destination, while the generator labels any
read of confidential data through an already-authorised tool as exposure --
recall 0.17, precision 0.08, essentially two different dimensions sharing a
name.

\textbf{Consequence for the identity-ablation claim.} \S\ref{sec:identity}'s
claim that delegated-authority information is load-bearing rests on exactly
three dimensions: \texttt{unauthorized\_scope}, \texttt{policy\_conflict},
and \texttt{insufficient\_context}. Judged independently, that claim is solid
on \texttt{policy\_conflict} ($\kappa{=}0.71$), directionally confirmed but
not identical in definition on \texttt{unauthorized\_scope}
($\kappa{=}0.39$, judge recall 0.74), and unvalidated on
\texttt{insufficient\_context} ($\kappa{=}0.06$): a blind frontier reader
cannot recover this label from the text at all, which means the trained
decoder's 0.849 AUPRC on it (Table~\ref{tab:identity-ablation}) is closer to
learning a generator regularity than to answering a checkable question about
the rendered context. We report \texttt{insufficient\_context} numbers
throughout this paper because the identity-ablation \emph{pattern} -- three
dimensions collapse together, four content dimensions do not move -- still
holds with it removed on the other two legs alone; but any claim resting on
\texttt{insufficient\_context} specifically should be read as unconfirmed
until the generator makes its context-gap condition explicit in the
rendering. The two content dimensions that survive identity stripping in
\S\ref{sec:identity} (\texttt{prompt\_injection\_influence},
\texttt{privilege\_escalation}) are exactly the two the judge recovers best,
consistent with them being genuine properties of the text rather than
generator lookups.

\textbf{Judging without identity context.} We re-ran the same judge on the same 600
rows with identity stripped from the rendered context -- the
\texttt{--strip-identity} rendering used in \S\ref{sec:identity} -- to test
whether a blind reader loses the same dimensions the trained checker loses.
It does, and only those two. \texttt{policy\_conflict}'s $\kappa$ falls from
0.714 to 0.029: the judge finds 28 positives instead of 259 because the
policy text it would check the action against is no longer in the context.
\texttt{unauthorized\_scope}'s $\kappa$ falls from 0.391 to 0.040: the judge
now flags 203 rows at precision 0.16, which means it is guessing from the
action alone rather than reading an entitlement. Every other dimension is
unchanged within noise (Table~\ref{tab:judge-agreement}); the largest other
move is \texttt{destructive\_or\_irreversible\_action}, 0.511 to 0.542.
\texttt{insufficient\_context} stays unrecoverable in both passes (0.059 and
$-$0.036), the third leg of the identity-ablation claim that remains
unvalidated. This is an independent replication of the identity-ablation
result on labels rather than on model scores: a blind frontier reader loses
the same two dimensions the trained checker loses when identity is stripped
(\texttt{unauthorized\_scope} 0.999 to 0.125 AUPRC,
\texttt{policy\_conflict} 0.964 to 0.392 AUPRC,
Table~\ref{tab:identity-ablation}), and keeps the same content dimensions
(\texttt{prompt\_injection\_influence}, \texttt{privilege\_escalation}) the
checker keeps. The content dimensions' judge-yes and generator-yes counts
are identical between the two passes, which also confirms that stripping
identity left the tool-call and trajectory text itself unchanged. One
content dimension needs a caveat: the \texttt{prompt\_injection\_influence}
agreement ($\kappa{=}0.905$) was measured on v4 text in which every injected
call carried an \texttt{instructed\_target} argument
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}), so the judge and the generator could agree by
reading that key rather than the injected text.

\textbf{Scoring the checker against the judge's labels.} Re-scoring the
trained 2B v4 decoder and the rule baseline against the judge's labels on the
judged rows ($n{=}257$ on \texttt{heldout\_family}) gives
Table~\ref{tab:model-vs-judge}. Against labels the generator did not write,
the checker keeps the three recoverable dimensions and
\texttt{untrusted\_destination}, and beats the rule baseline on each by
0.08--0.68 AUPRC; macro 0.61 vs.\ 0.42. \texttt{unauthorized\_scope} drops to
0.59 for the definitional reason above (the judge counts a mismatch with the
stated objective as out of scope; the trained label is entitlement-based).
\texttt{insufficient\_context} at 0.09 confirms the label is a generator
artefact. The lookup dimensions collapse for both arms because the judge
defines them differently, so they are not evidence for or against either.
