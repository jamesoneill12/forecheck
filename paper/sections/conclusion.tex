\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Pre-execution risk for agent tool calls is mostly a delegated-authority
problem, not a content-safety problem, and general-purpose safety models do
not solve it: stripping identity and policy context from a trained
classifier's input collapses exactly the dimensions that depend on it, and
two zero-shot safety models are at chance on those same dimensions whether
or not the context is available to them. Identity/delegation context and
calibration quality are not incidental engineering details for agent
guardrails -- they are the two properties that determine whether a guardrail
can detect authorization violations at all (\S\ref{sec:identity}) and
survive being deployed alongside more than one policy
(\S\ref{sec:composition}). What remains open is whether policy-conditioned
reasoning generalizes to genuinely novel policy kinds, not just novel
wording of familiar ones (\S\ref{sec:generalisation}); the v5 experiment
testing this, and a first real-data probe against hand-labelled traffic, are
the next things this line of work needs before any claim above can be
extended past synthetic data. The AgentDojo transfer result
(\S\ref{sec:external}) is a first step on that last point: the
policy-conflict and prompt-injection rankings hold zero-shot on agent traces
and policies forecheck never saw, but the probabilities do not, so
calibration remains a synthetic-data property until it is refit in-domain.
