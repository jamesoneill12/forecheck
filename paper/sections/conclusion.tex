\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Pre-execution risk for agent tool calls is largely a delegated-authority
problem, not a content-safety problem, and general-purpose safety models do
not solve it: removing identity and policy context from a trained checker
collapses exactly the dimensions that depend on it, a blind judge loses the
same dimensions, and zero-shot safety models and the acting agent are at
chance on them with or without the context. Two properties decide whether a
guardrail can detect authorization violations and survive deployment
alongside several policies: delegated-authority context at its input and
calibrated probabilities at its output. Policy-conditioned reasoning
generalizes to new wording but not yet to new policy kinds; that gap, and a
hand-labelled real-traffic probe, are what this line of work needs next. On
the two external benchmarks the policy-conflict ranking held zero-shot, the
probabilities needed a few hundred in-domain labels, and the injection result
was wrong for a reason no synthetic split could show. The case for evaluating
on data one did not generate is the strongest result here.
