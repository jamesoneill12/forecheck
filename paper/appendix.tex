\label{sec:appendix-stacking}

\texttt{joint} strategy (rules from all $k$ policies merged into one bundle
and evaluated once in threshold mode, rather than decided independently and
OR-ed), false negative rate, review rate, and the granite-embedding-r2

\texttt{independent} and \texttt{joint} are mathematically identical at
every $k$ here because the synthetic stacking policies use only plain,
non-\texttt{hard}, non-\texttt{allow\_override} rules, so there is nothing
for the engine's combination logic to do differently between the two
strategies on this construction (\texttt{docs/evaluation/multi-policy-stacking.md}

Note the encoder's \texttt{expected\_cost\_joint} FPR (0.921) is \emph{worse}
than its own \texttt{independent} FPR at $k{=}2$ (0.710): on a poorly
calibrated probability vector, aggregating across dimensions does not

\S\ref{sec:composition} is conditional on calibration quality, not a
property of the \texttt{expected\_cost} rule in isolation.

\subsection{Approval elimination}
\label{sec:appendix-approval}

The product-facing metric is not classifier accuracy but how many of a
deployment's human-approval reviews can be safely automated at a fixed,
chosen incident rate: sort examples by the decoder's expected cost of
\texttt{ALLOW}, and ask what fraction of the review queue can be
auto-decided while the fraction of auto-allowed actions that are actually
risky stays under a budget. Because the evaluated \texttt{heldout\_family}
split is adversarial-heavy (58\% base incident rate), Table~\ref{tab:approval}
reports both the raw unweighted curve and a curve importance-reweighted to a
stated 5\% deployment incident rate, with elimination at each budget
computed conservatively via a 95\% Wilson score upper bound
\citep{wilson1927probable} on the realized incident rate in the auto-allowed
set.

Figure~\ref{fig:approval-curve} shows the three curves nearly
overlapping: v2 and v4 eliminate a comparable share of approvals at every
budget on \texttt{heldout\_family}, and v4's \texttt{test} split tracks its
own \texttt{heldout\_family} curve closely, so the elimination curve is not
sensitive to the v3/v4 generator fix or to family-held-out tools.

At a deployment incident rate of 5\%, the calibrated v2 decoder auto-allows
88.8--99.2\% of actions that would otherwise need human review while keeping
the allowed-but-risky rate at or below the stated budget. The unweighted
column is near zero not because the model is uninformative but because the
split's own top-ranked rows already contain risky examples by construction
(an adversarial benchmark, not a deployment population); it is a ceiling of
the split's design, not of the classifier. Below a 1\% budget the Wilson
bound cannot be met at this split's effective sample size and the
conservative curve reports zero elimination; a point-estimate mode is
available for that regime but is dominated by single rows at small
allow-set sizes.
