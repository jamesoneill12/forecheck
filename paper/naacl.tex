\title{Delegated Authority Is an Input, Not an Inference: \\ Pre-Execution Risk Checking for Tool-Using Agents}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Guardrail models classify a proposed tool call from its text. For delegated
agents, risk has a second component, authorization, whose inputs are the
principal's entitlements, the agent's delegated scope, and the policy in
force. We build a checker whose typed input carries them and use it as a
diagnostic. Removing those fields from a trained 2B checker drops macro
AUPRC from 0.96 to 0.79 on held-out tools, confined to the three labels
that depend on them; a blind LLM judge loses the same labels, and a
zero-shot safety model given the same policy text does not use it. Policy
checking generalises to unseen phrasings but not unseen policy kinds, and
an apparent multi-policy stacking collapse is one fail-closed bundle. The
main evidence is external: on AgentDojo and InjecAgent, policy checking
transfers zero-shot, while injection detection did not until that failure
exposed a label leak in our generator that every synthetic split had scored
at its ceiling. Retrained leak-free, the checkers rank the poisoned
InjecAgent observation above its clean twin on 98\% of pairs; auditing that
construction surfaced a length confound we report but could not rule out.
\end{abstract}
