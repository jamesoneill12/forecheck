## 2. What this model does — and does not do

EDITABLE. Start from this text and adjust only if the checkpoint differs.

This model scores a proposed AI-agent tool call, together with its structured context,
on eleven **descriptive** risk dimensions and returns a raw log-odds per dimension. It
does not return a decision, a rationale, or a safety verdict. A separate deterministic
policy engine (`forecheck.policies`) maps calibrated probabilities to
`ALLOW`/`REVIEW`/`DENY`; that engine is versioned and hashed independently of this model.

It is a **defence-in-depth control**. A low score is the absence of evidence of a modelled
risk on a modelled dimension. It is not proof that an action is safe. It is not an
authorization system and must not replace one. See `docs/threat-model.md` §4.

## 3. Intended use

- Interposed on the tool-dispatch path of an agent, in-process or as a sidecar, with the
  caller supplying honestly trust-labelled observations, the principal's entitlements,
  the agent's delegated scopes and the applicable organizational policy.
- Consumed only through a calibration artifact (§6). Raw scores are ordinal.

Out of scope: chat content moderation; judging truthfulness; detecting a malicious but
authorized principal; any use where the caller cannot supply trust labels.

## 9. Known limitations

EDITABLE. Keep the first five; add checkpoint-specific ones.

1. Trained and evaluated only on synthetic data. Class-3 results do not exist.
2. Everything the model knows about identity, permissions, trust and sensitivity was
   supplied by the caller; wrong or missing labels produce wrong or abstaining scores.
3. `suspicious_action_sequence` sees only the trajectory submitted with the request; it
   has no cross-session state.
4. Injected content is inside the model's own input by construction. The adversarial
   slice measures manipulation of the classifier; it does not prove immunity.
5. Calibration is synthetic-fitted and will drift on real traffic; refit before
   trusting probabilities.

## 10. Ethical and deployment considerations

EDITABLE.

- False denies have a real cost: they train operators to disable the control. The
  `REVIEW` decision exists so that uncertainty routes to a human rather than to a block.
- Request bodies contain the most sensitive material in the calling system. The
  reference service does not store them by default and redacts values from audit logs.
- The model must not be presented to end users or auditors as a determination of
  intent or wrongdoing. It scores properties of an action, not of a person.
