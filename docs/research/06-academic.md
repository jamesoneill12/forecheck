# Primary-source scan: academic literature on pre-execution AI-agent action guarding (2023-2026)

Date of research: 2026-09-20. Scope: peer-reviewed and arXiv-preprint literature (2023-2026) on guarding AI-agent tool calls before execution, benchmarked against our proposed **vendor-neutral, sequence-aware pre-execution risk classifier for AI-agent tool calls** — typed, calibrated per-dimension probabilities across 11 atomic risk dimensions (prompt_injection_influence, unauthorized_scope, sensitive_data_exposure, untrusted_destination, privilege_escalation, destructive_or_irreversible_action, financial_commitment, external_communication, policy_conflict, suspicious_action_sequence, insufficient_context), with a **separate deterministic policy engine** mapping outputs to ALLOW/REVIEW/DENY.

Method: arXiv abstract pages/API, official repos, and (where WebSearch quota allowed) publisher/blog pages fetched directly. Where the shared session WebSearch budget was exhausted mid-scan, research fell back to direct `arxiv.org`/`export.arxiv.org` API queries and abstract-page fetches — still primary source, but with narrower recall than full-text/multi-engine search (proceedings sites for USENIX Security/IEEE S&P/CCS/NDSS were not separately crawled beyond what arXiv preprints surface). Every claim below carries a direct URL. Numbers not directly readable from the source are marked "number not verified." Named systems not found are marked **NOT FOUND** with the queries attempted.

## The specific open gap

Based on the above scan, forecheck's proposed combination should be described precisely as follows — conservatively, without overstating novelty on any single axis:

**Already well-covered by prior art (individually):**
- **Pre-execution gating of agent tool calls** is a crowded, fast-moving area (CaMeL, ROPE, Progent, AgentSpec, ActGuard, AEGIS, TraceGrant, ShieldAgent, `agent-guard-python`, `agentguard-security`) — the *timing* (before execution) is not novel.
- **Deterministic policy/authorization engines separate from a detection step** are common (Progent, AgentSpec, CaMeL's capabilities, ROPE's origin-tracing, TraceGrant's contracts, and the PyPI packages) — the *architectural split* between a decision signal and a policy layer is not novel, though most existing systems fuse the two into one deterministic pass/fail rather than a two-stage classifier→policy pipeline.
- **Sequence/trajectory-aware analysis** is an active, if very recent (2025-2026), sub-literature (TrajAD, Trajectory Guard, SafeAgent, Enforcing Benign Trajectories, AgentSentry, ShieldAgent, AgentArmor) — scoring more than a single action is not novel in isolation, though the literature itself still splits between step-level and trajectory-level systems, so this remains a genuine differentiator versus roughly half of comparable systems, not versus all of them.
- **Calibration methods (temperature scaling, isotonic-adjacent, conformal prediction) applied to safety/moderation classifiers** are established (ICLR 2025 guard-model calibration paper, Safety-Flag, ProbGuard-calibration, several conformal-prediction papers) — calibration technique itself is not novel.
- **Identity/permission-conditioned action gating** exists both academically (Progent, ShieldAgent, ROPE, CaMeL) and as an off-the-shelf library (`open-guard-python`).
- **Synthetic/programmatic contrastive-pair training data for a safety-adjacent classifier** has direct precedent in StruQ and SecAlign (programmatically constructed secure-vs-insecure pairs for the *same* input), and in the broader Snorkel/counterfactual-augmentation lineage — the *general technique* is not novel, though its application to atomic per-dimension agent-tool-call risk labels specifically was not found.

**Not found covered anywhere in this scan, i.e. the actual open gap:**
- **No system combines all of**: (1) pre-execution, (2) per-action (not whole-session) *and* sequence-aware scoring in the same model, (3) a fixed taxonomy of **multiple independent atomic risk dimensions** scored simultaneously (vs. a single scalar/binary risk label — the closest analogs, Semalith v1.4's 22-class head and AgentHarm's 11 harm categories, are multi-*label* but not shown to be independently *calibrated* probabilities), (4) **calibrated** probabilities specifically for that multi-dimensional pre-execution agent-action setting (calibration work exists only for single-label content moderation or single-scalar agent risk scores — ProbGuard/DTMC and ProbGuard-calibration are the closest, both single-scalar), (5) identity/permission/policy-conditioning of the *classifier's* probability estimate itself (existing identity-aware systems like Progent/ROPE/CaMeL/`open-guard-python` use identity/policy only in a deterministic symbolic check, not as a conditioning input to a learned probability), and (6) full open release of **weights + training data + training recipe** together (every academic system found either releases code-only, benchmark-only, or nothing; `agent-guard-python`/`agentguard-security`/`open-guard-python` are open-source rules engines, not open ML artifacts).
- **No paper or package was found that calibrates a multi-dimensional (independent-per-dimension) probability vector for pre-execution agent-tool-call risk.** This is the most defensible specific claim from this scan — verified by an explicit closing check in sub-area (d) across ~15 calibration papers, none of which touch a multi-dimensional agent-action setting.
- **No academic system explicitly benchmarks or claims low latency plus calibrated multi-dimensional probabilities plus a separate deterministic policy layer as one design** — the closest analog (AuraGen/Safiron/Pre-Exec Bench) is explicitly pre-execution and multi-step but outputs risk-type + rationale, not calibrated probabilities; the closest calibration analog (ProbGuard, Semalith) does not decompose into fixed atomic dimensions or address a separate deterministic policy stage.

**Caveats on this gap claim**: this is a fast-moving space — five of the sub-area (a)-(c) systems found were published in the six weeks immediately preceding this scan (Aug-Sep 2026: ROPE, ActGuard, FLOWSEAL, TraceGrant, SkillAtlas), and the WebSearch quota constraint meant proceedings sites (USENIX Security, IEEE S&P, CCS, NDSS) were not crawled beyond what surfaces as an arXiv preprint — a paper published only in a proceedings PDF without an arXiv mirror would not have been found. The gap claim above should be treated as "not found in this scan," not as a certified absence, and re-verified against a fresh search closer to any publication date.
